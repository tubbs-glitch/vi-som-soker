The stair begins in the dark. You set your hand to the rail without seeing it. It is cold.

You take a step. Creak. Second step. Creak.

You stop. Not because you have decided to. You have only stopped.

There is a sound up there. Not loud. It is not feet and it is not wind. It is something that moves slowly, heavily, a second at a time.

You stand there, one hand on the rail, one against the wall. The wallpaper uneven under your finger.

You breathe in. You breathe out. You don't hear your own breath against the sound up there.

Then it stops. Not slowly. It is there, and then it is not there. As though something has lain down.

You remain. You remain longer than you need to. From the window at the top comes the blue dusk light. It shines upward on the wall, not down on the steps. You don't recall seeing light do that.

## In the torch's beam

The cone falls on the rail first. Then on the steps. You count them as you go — one, two, three. The fourth creaks. The fifth creaks. Between them lies a worn strip of carpet pinned with small brass tacks. Two of the tacks are gone. The beam slides over them.

Halfway up you stop. The cone rests on the sixth step.

There is a sound up there. The beam is on the treads, not raised. You don't move it. You don't move it.

You breathe in. You breathe out.

Then the sound stops. As though something has lain down.

You raise the beam. It reaches halfway up the stair, then the light is too thin. You don't see the window at the top. You only see that the light beyond the cone is blue, not yellow.

You take the last steps. The last step does not creak. The fifth — the second from the top, if you are counting downward — should have creaked. It did not, this time.

## When the power is on

The staircase is narrower than it looked from below. It creaks on the fourth step and the fifth. Between them lies a worn strip of carpet pinned down with small brass tacks. Two of the tacks are gone.

Halfway up you stop. Not because you have decided to. You have only stopped.

There is a sound up there. Not loud. It is not feet and it is not wind. It is something that moves slowly, heavily, a second at a time, as if a thing were shifting itself from one place to almost the same place.

You stand with one hand on the rail and the other on the wall. The rail is cool although the house is not cool. You notice that.

You breathe in. You breathe out. You don't hear your own breath against the sound up there.

Then the sound stops.

It does not stop slowly. It is there, and then it is not there. As though something has lain down.

You remain on the stair. You remain longer than you need to. The sun through the small window at the top of the stairs stands at the wrong angle — it shines upward on the wall, not down onto the steps, you don't recall seeing light do that, or you don't recall noticing it before.

The ceiling lamp above the stair is on. A yellow light over the worn treads. The stair does not look any less wrong for it.

Your watch reads twenty-three fourteen. You remember Gunnar said the sun would not go down tonight. You remember he said it without pride and without fear, only said it.

You take the last steps.

The last step does not creak. The fifth — the second from the top, if you are counting downward — should have creaked. It did not, this time.

## Back in the room

You climb the stair again. The fourth and fifth steps creak. The second from the top — it didn't creak the first time. You don't think about it. You go on.
