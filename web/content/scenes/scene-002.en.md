It is twenty past two. You stand in the hall with your jacket in your hand and you decide not to sit down, because if you sit down you sleep. The flat is hot and still. Outside, the buses have stopped running.

You take the small case from the wardrobe. Small is good. What you take should be things you already know how to use.

The torch is on the hat shelf where it belongs. Rubber-cased, heavy in the hand. You used it last summer when the power went, and it gave a clean white beam without flicker.

The hunting knife — your father's — is in the drawer of the telephone table. You took it when he died. You have carried it once on an elk hunt and once never. The sheath still smells of leather and machine oil.

On the bookshelf, behind the albums, sits the cassette recorder from eighty-six. You put it there once and have not moved it since. It still works, you know that without testing.

The phone charger is where it lives.

The first-aid kit is from a workplace handout last autumn. Inadequately small. Plasters, a roll of gauze, a strip of silver tape.

Warm clothes. You remember that Hällmyren is north. You pull out a wool jumper and a rain jacket. You remember Alice saying something about the gnats in June.

In the kitchen drawer there is a ten-pack of AA batteries. You do not know why you have so many.

You stand with the things laid out around you. You can take five. Not six. You decide now, so that you will not change your mind later.
