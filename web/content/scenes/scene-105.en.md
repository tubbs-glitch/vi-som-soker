You go back the way you came, only heavier. Saw in one hand, axe in the other or over your shoulder, whichever you took. The midges do not care that you carry iron. The midges do not care about anything at all.

The spruce lies where it lay. Only you have changed in between.

You set yourself at the trunk where it is narrowest — where the branches begin to thin toward the crown, a little above the line itself. That point is enough. You do not need to take the whole tree. You only need to take enough trunk for the end of the cable to come free.

The saw bites slowly at first. Then the blade finds its line. You have not sawed in a long time but the body remembers anyway — forward, back, forward, back, the shoulder does it, not the arm. You settle to a rhythm. Sawdust falls across your shoe. It smells of fresh spruce and resin, a smell that does not belong at midnight and is here anyway. You hear your own breath, even, a little heavy.

The cut grows. Two thirds in, you take the axe and drive a wedge into the underside. The tree behaves. It twists very slightly. You saw the last centimetres. The trunk gives with a sound that is not crack and not snap but a long opening of grain, and the upper half of the spruce rolls a half metre to one side and stops.

The cable moves. Not much — only the way a string draws itself straight when the weight comes off. It slides off the iron bracket on the pole and hangs again the way it is meant to hang, slightly slack but free, off the metal. The pole still stands. It leans less now.

You think of the fuse. It fell back every time you tried. There is nothing pulling it down now.

You walk to the shed. You open the plate. You take the main lever. You take one breath. You push it up.

The lever stays up.

Not a sound comes from the shed. Not a sound comes from the garden. But inside the house — you hear it before you turn — something begins. A low hum from a lamp. Then another. Then, from the open kitchen window down at the corner, something begins to turn. A platter. Only the platter.

You leave the axe by the shed door. You walk toward the house.
