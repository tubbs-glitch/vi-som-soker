The upper hall is short. The blue night light comes in through a window at the far end, the casement open a finger's breadth. You see the shapes of the doors in the dark around it.

Four doors. You count them with your eyes. A narrower hatch in the ceiling — only a blacker square than the ceiling around it.

The wallpaper is uneven under your finger when you set your hand to the wall. You sense a floral print without seeing it.

You listen.

You hear nothing now. You heard something a moment ago. You know which door it was behind. The unmarked one.

You stand on a rug. You don't know how wide it is.

## In the torch's beam

The cone falls on the rug at your feet. Then on the first door. You bring the beam up to the sign. Brass mount, enamel. *Alice.*

You move the beam on. Second door. *Guest.* Third door. *Bath.* Fourth door — no sign. Behind the unmarked one you know there is a stair to the attic. You know it before opening anything.

You raise the beam to the wall between two of the doors. It finds an oval frame. A portrait. A woman in her forties, dark clothes, large spectacles. You don't know her. It is one of the faces from the group photograph in Leopold's study, if you saw it — the one without the cross.

You lift the beam to the far window. It reaches. The casement is open. Through it comes the blue.

You lower the beam back to the rug. You stand there.

## When the power is on

The upper hall is short. Four doors and a narrower hatch in the ceiling. The wallpaper is blue, ancient, the floral print almost bleached out at chest height where a hand has run along it across the years.

The north window at the far end stands open just a finger's breadth. Through the gap comes the blue night light. It is not a warm light. It shines although the hour is what it is.

A wall lamp between the doors is on. The glow is yellow and the left side of the wallpaper is no longer blue — it is faded green and worn, as it has always been.

On the wall between two of the doors hangs a portrait in an oval frame. A woman in her forties, dark clothes, a photograph from the seventies or early eighties. You don't know her. She wears large spectacles. It is one of the faces from the group photograph in Leopold's study, if you saw it — the one without the cross.

The doors are marked with small enamelled signs in low brass mounts: *Alice. Guest. Bath.* One door is not marked. Behind that one there is a stair to the attic — you know it before opening anything.

You listen. You hear nothing now. You heard something a moment ago. You know which door it was behind.

It was the unmarked one.

You stand on the rug and think you have chosen already without knowing, and then you think you have not.

## Back in the room

You are back in the upper hall. Four doors — *Alice*, *Guest*, *Bath*, and the unmarked one to the attic. The portrait on the wall between two of them. The window at the far end open a finger's breadth.
