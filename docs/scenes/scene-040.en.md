You are standing in the cellar.

The circle is no longer there. The tjärsten lies where it was, but the geometrical marks are gone — ground out. You did not grind them. It is a room that is a room now.

Alice is beside you. She has one hand on the cellar wall. She breathes in once and out.

*Thank you*, she says.

You do not know whether she is saying it to you or to what is no longer there.

Leopold stands leaning against the doorframe. He is sleeping on his feet, perhaps. He has done it before.

The record is still turning up above. You go up and lift the needle. You take the disc off the platter. You hold it a moment. It is the 1962 pressing from East Germany. The only copy that exists.

You walk out. You go to the shed. You take the axe from where you left it.

You do not do that either. You go back in. You slip the disc into its sleeve and the sleeve into a drawer in Leopold's study. You shut the drawer. You stand with your hand on it a while.

---

*Three months later. Late summer. The bog.*

You walk out to the stand of birches. Six hundred metres, the same line of planks as before. The midges are gone. It is September.

The gravestone still leans. But under the first line — *Bertil Lindblom — 1935 — Midsummer 1979* — someone has cut a second line. It is new. It is clean stonework, small letters, but deep.

*Midsummer 2026.*

It is not the burial of a body. It is a date. He has two dates now.

You did not cut it. You do not know who did.

You stand there. You gave Gunnar the note the day before yesterday. He read it. He did not say much. He said he had always wondered why his father had stopped laughing after the summer of 1981.

You walk back. You do not walk quickly.
