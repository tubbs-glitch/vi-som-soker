He has walked back toward his cottage. You stood for a moment and watched his back disappear among the birches. He did not turn.

It is twenty past ten in the evening. The sun sits low over the fell but it will not go down tonight. It will not go down until somewhere into August. You have always read about the midnight sun as a curiosity. You have not thought of it as something that would apply to you.

The key is under the flagstone where he said it would be. You bend and take it. It is cold. It has lain there a long time.

You go up the three steps. They creak the way old steps creak — not like an alarm, more like a confirmation. You stand at the door. You put the key in the lock.

You do not need to turn it. The door is not locked.

You stand with your hand on the handle and think that this is a moment you will remember. Not because anything dreadful happens just now. Because it is the last moment before.

You press down on the handle.
