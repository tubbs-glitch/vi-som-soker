You stand at the door under the stair. It is low. You have to dip your head. You set your hand to the latch without seeing it — iron, smooth, warmed by fifty years of hands.

Behind you the hall is dark enough. Here is something else. A colder air.

You take hold of the latch. It does not give at once — damp in the wood has pulled it crooked. Then it gives.

Behind the door, a dark that even the blue dusk outside had not changed. A different dark.

You feel cold air rising. Clay, something sour, pine tar far off.

You see not one of the treads.

## In the torch's beam

The cone falls on the doorframe first. Then into the opening.

The stair is stone. The beam finds the top two treads. The third you lose — the light thins and is swallowed by something clay-dark. You don't move the beam further.

You set your hand to the doorframe. The cold air comes against you now, even and low.

You stand with one foot on the first step. The cone reaches only as far as it reaches.

## When the power is on

The door to the cellar sits under the upstairs stair, low, low enough that you have to dip your head. The latch is iron and worn smooth by a hand that has held it for fifty years. You take hold of it. It does not give at once — damp in the wood has pulled it crooked — and then it gives.

Behind the door the dark begins at once. It is the only real dark in the house tonight. Up above, everything is blue. Here, everything is only black. There is no lamp in the ceiling above the stair — only a cable ending in nothing.

You stand with one hand on the doorframe. Cold air rises from the earth cellar below. It smells of clay and something sour that is not food and not mould, something older. It smells of pine tar very far off.

The stair is stone. You can see the top two treads from the light behind you. The third you lose.

## Back in the room

You stand again at the cellar door. Iron latch, low door under the upstairs stair. The cold air comes against you. You dip your head.
