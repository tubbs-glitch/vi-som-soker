The bathroom is small and dark. The long window above the bath lets in a slit of blue. The tiles around it gleam faintly.

You hear the tap first. It drips. Unevenly. You stand in the doorway and try to find the rhythm — and as soon as you think you have it, it skips.

It smells of cold damp and soap and something else — dry and animal — dog, old dog.

You sense the shape of the bath. The basin against the wall. The mirror a black square. You see nothing in it.

Something hums faintly at the window. Gnats. Many. They settle on your forearm. You do not wave them away.

## In the torch's beam

The cone finds the tap first. White porcelain, a drop hanging a second too long before it falls. It drips again. You move the beam.

On the rim of the basin lies a hairbrush. Walnut handle. Long grey hair in the bristles, more than any one brushing should reasonably gather. You don't pick it up.

You raise the beam to the mirror. A crack up in one corner that nobody has bothered to replace. You see yourself in the light — you and only you. You don't look long.

The beam moves to the bath. Claw feet. Thin rings of rust where they meet the floor. At the foot of the bath stand a pair of socks. Brown wool. She was here before she wasn't.

You lift the beam to the window. The catch is open — four centimetres, no more. The gnats come in through the gap, one at a time, into the beam and out again.

## When the power is on

The bathroom is small, lined with white tiles from the seventies with a blue trim along the upper edge. A clawfoot bath, a basin with a porcelain plug, a mirror with a crack up in one corner that nobody has bothered to replace. It smells of cold damp and soap and something else — dry and animal — dog perhaps, old dog, although there has not been a dog in the house for years.

The tap drips. The drip is uneven. You stand for a while listening — you try to find the rhythm — and as soon as you think you have it, it skips.

On the rim of the basin lies a hairbrush. The handle is walnut. There is long grey hair in the bristles, more than any one brushing should reasonably gather. You don't pick it up.

Through the long window above the bath — the window is open on its catch, four centimetres, no more — the gnats find their way in. They are many now. Not a cloud, only many. They settle on the mirror, on the rim of the basin, on your forearm. You do not wave them away.

On the floorboard at the foot of the bath stand a pair of socks. Brown wool. She was here before she wasn't.

There is nobody in the mirror but you. You don't look long.

The strip light above the mirror comes on with a faint click and blinks twice before holding. The white light shows the grout between the tiles more sharply than the blue night light did.

## Back in the room

You are back in the bathroom. The tap still drips unevenly. The hairbrush on the rim of the basin, the socks at the foot of the bath. The gnats find their way in.
