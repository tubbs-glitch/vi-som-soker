You are standing in the cellar.

You are standing in the cellar and you are not standing in anything grey. It smells of limestone and pine-tar pointing and something else — a smell that has been absent from this room since you were small, the smell of two people who have lived together for decades. You are standing at the centre of the circle. You are whole. Alice is beside you. Leopold is beside Alice. He is holding her hand. She lets him hold it.

The record needle skips. You hear it above, one ratchet, then silence. The power flickers — the cellar lamp comes on, dies, comes on. Then steady.

You go up.

It is light outside. It is three in the morning and the sun is on the mountain. You walk through the hall. On the empty hook — the seventh hook that has been empty — there is a collar now. You did not hang it there. You know that.

You walk out onto the steps. Gunnar is on his own step four hundred metres off. He sees you. He does not raise his hand, but he sees you.

---

*Three months later. Late summer. Alice's kitchen.*

She is planting tulips for autumn. It is the wrong season — she knows and you know — but she plants them anyway. She does not smile. She sets the bulbs down in an order she has chosen herself. Leopold sits on the kitchen bench and drinks coffee from the blue enamel mug.

He does not say much. He says two sentences a day. One is good morning. The other varies.

In the attic you sometimes hear steps. You do not check. The feeding bowl by the hatch is always empty in the mornings.

You think your mother will come up in September. You have rung her. She does not know what she is going to say. She is coming anyway.
