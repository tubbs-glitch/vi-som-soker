They went through.

That is the first thing you have to say to yourself so that you can stand. They went. Alice and Leopold went. You heard Alice say your name just before the echo thinned. She did not say it with gratitude. She said it because she understood.

You stand where you stand.

The grey turns toward you.

It says your name. It is still your voice. But it is not your name any more.

*Thank you*, it says, in your voice, as though the voice were learning the word for the first time. It says it again. And again.

The grey is not thicker now. It is only with you. It is a room that is not empty.

---

*(If `talat_med_ingegerd=true`:)*

You see her out of the corner of your eye.

She stands with her back turned, as she has always stood. She does not turn her head any further than she has ever turned it. But she puts out a hand. Not toward a face — not her face and not yours. She lays the hand across your shoulder. It is not heavy. It is not light either.

She says nothing. There is nothing to say.

You are less alone.

---

*(If `talat_med_ingegerd=false`:)*

You are alone with the grey.

You stand there a long time. You stand there until long time is no longer a thing.

You think once of Alice. You think once of your mother. You think once that the coffee mug is still standing on the draining board in your flat in Stockholm. The glass is still half full.

Then you stop thinking in rows.

---

In the house, on the other side, Alice and Leopold sit quiet at the kitchen table. They do not say your name. They do not say any name. They know someone stayed so they could come home, and they know who.

Alice sets two cups. She drinks from only one.
